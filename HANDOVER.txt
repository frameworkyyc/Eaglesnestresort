HERO PAPER RIDGE (THINGS TO DO)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The next section's paper, cut to a mountain skyline and laid over
the foot of the hero — not a divider between two bands. Fill is
var(--paper), the same colour as the section below, and the element
sits flush at bottom:-1px, so there is no seam and no straight edge
anywhere between hero and section.

It paints above .stay-hero-in so the paper covers the scrim rather
than the scrim muddying the paper. Peaks rise inside the hero box,
so .stay-hero's overflow:hidden never clips them.

Two things the default hero fought, both fixed for this page only:
padding the copy clear of the ridge stretched the scrim box to
nearly the full hero height and washed the engraving grey; and the
scrim's hard bottom edge drew a straight horizontal line across the
image between the peaks. The scrim is now shorter and fades back to
nothing at its foot, with the tail landing inside the ridge band
where the paper hides it.

Ridge height is clamp(88px,12vw,180px) — about 37% of the hero on a
desktop, 20% on a phone. Raising it much past that starts swallowing
the sun and the lake.

Only .stay-hero.ttd is affected. Every other hero on the site keeps
the original scrim.


SCROLL FURNITURE ON THINGS TO DO
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Two extra skyline transitions, both using the existing .ridge
component rather than a new mechanism — same two-layer far/near
paths, same --near/--far colour hooks, same negative-margin overlap.
Only the path data is new.

  after the dock section    a conifer treeline, with a bear
  after the waterfalls      mountain peaks, with a deer

The animals cannot live inside those SVGs: the skylines use
preserveAspectRatio="none" so they stretch to any width, and a
silhouette would stretch with them. Each sits on top instead, as a
.ridge-fig span with its own aspect ratio. Both offsets are
percentages, and since the skyline geometry scales proportionally
in both axes, a figure placed on a valley floor stays on that
valley floor at every viewport width.

The treeline carries a deliberate clearing between x=150 and x=330
of its 1440 viewBox. That is where the bear stands, so it reads
against the far trees instead of disappearing into the near ones.
If the bear moves, the clearing has to move with it.

A tent and a campfire sit under the After Dark copy, in .ttd-camp,
gold at half opacity.

All five silhouettes were rasterised and looked at before shipping.
Worth doing: the first bear read as a pig, and an early extraction
bug had leaked Python comment text into the SVG markup, which would
have rendered as visible words on the page.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
Booking in the mobile menu. The .nav-cta button is display:none
below 1000px, so on a phone the header offered no route to booking

so the white glyph sits on the dark footer rather than in a box.

rather than flex space-between, which had stranded the address
against the right edge. The right column is given a definite width,
clamp(320px,31%,380px), and the gap does the rest — across the real
range of widths it starts between 67% and 69% across and runs

footer navigation instead of level with the wordmark; it tracks the
same vw as the wordmark's own clamp so the two stay in step.

The build credit sits 2.4rem below the address and slightly dimmer,
reading as a signature rather than another line of resort detail.

Below 900px nothing applies and the footer stacks as before. The
photo credit line has been removed from all pages.


QUICK FACTS GRID
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The panel no longer uses a fixed column count. It counts its own
items with :has(> div:nth-child(N):last-child) and picks a column
figure that splits them without stranding one on its own: 8 -> 4+4,
7 -> 4+3, 9 -> 5+4 on desktop. Nothing names an accommodation.

Two width bands, deliberately non-overlapping: 900px and up, and
621-899px where cells are narrower so counts of 9 and above drop to
three per row. Below 621px the old two-column mobile panel is
untouched.

Separators are per-cell, not full-height. Each block clears the top
border on its first row, the right border on the last cell of each
row, and the right border on the very last cell — that final rule
has to live inside each block, because the shared last-child rule
is less specific than the border re-assert and would lose to it.

Adding or removing a fact re-balances the panel on its own. If a
count above 14 ever appears, add a line to the map in the generator
(gen_quickfacts.py, kept outside the site folder) and regenerate,
or the panel falls back to auto-fit.

Browsers without :has() get the auto-fit fallback: less tidy, still
correct.


COLLAPSED GALLERY PREVIEW
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The collapsed resort gallery is the real gallery, cropped. The
masonry is untouched — same columns, same widths, same image sizes,
captions intact. A wrapper div.gal-clip around it carries the
.collapsed class, a max-height and overflow:hidden, plus a gradient
::after easing the cut into the page. Expanding lifts the crop.

The height must sit on that wrapper, not on .masonry. A multi-column
box given a fixed height does not crop, it fragments: the overflow
spills into further columns sideways and you get the whole gallery
in thin strips instead of the top of it. This was tried and was
wrong. Constrain a plain block parent instead.

Preview heights: 240-320px mobile, 300-400px from 700px, 340-460px
from 1100px. Tune these to taste; nothing else depends on them.

site.js was not touched. It reads getElementById("resort-gallery"),
which is now the wrapper rather than the masonry, and toggles the
class on it. aria-controls still resolves.

The gradient hard-codes --paper as rgba(243,238,225,...). If the
gallery section ever moves onto a different band colour, that
gradient needs a matching override or the fade will show as a
pale block.

Cost of cropping rather than hiding: all 34 thumbnails now lay out
and load, where the old collapsed state used display:none and
fetched only the visible few. Roughly 2 MB on a page visit. Hiding
the far-below items would fix it but would also reshuffle the
masonry balance and change which photographs appear in the visible
row, so it has not been done.

DEPLOYMENT
------------------------------------------------------------------
Upload the whole folder, not just the .html files. css, js and
images must go up together.

The stylesheet address carries a hash of the css and js content
(?v=ffe03361 at the time of writing), so it changes only when
those files change. _headers tells Cloudflare to revalidate css
and js on every request.

If the site ever looks unstyled after a deploy, that is almost
always an old stylesheet still being served. Open css/site.css on
the live URL and search it for ".accom" — if it is not there, the
stylesheet did not upload.
