THE MAP SHEET (THINGS TO DO)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The hand-built SVG paper ridge is gone. In its place the owner's
illustrated map page, images/map-sheet.webp, is the background of
the first section, and its drawn mountains rise out of the section
to overlap the foot of the hero.

It is an <img>, not a CSS background, so it keeps its aspect ratio
at every width and never stretches.

One number does the work:
  --map-peaks    3vw, clamped 20-60px
  --map-overlap  calc(31.5vw + var(--map-peaks))

The sheet has an empty margin above its art. On the current artwork
the peaks start 21% down a file 1.5x its own width, so that margin
is 31.5vw. Writing the overlap as margin + --map-peaks makes
--map-peaks literally how far the peaks rise into the hero, so it
is the only value to touch. The hero copy is held clear of it too.

REPLACE THE ARTWORK AND THE 31.5vw MUST BE RE-MEASURED: multiply
the fraction of the file above the art by the sheet's height-to-
width ratio. Get it wrong and the mountains stop short, leaving a
flat band under the hero. That has happened once already, and the
figure has changed twice — it was 12.5vw on the previous sheet.

The paper is #F0D5B4. On it the site's gold drops to 2.1:1, so the
eyebrow was barely legible — true of the previous artwork too. A
darker gold, #7C5414, is scoped to this section only.

The sheet's paper is much warmer than --paper: about #EFD9B8
against the site's #F3EEE1. The section's background is set to that
warmer tone, and the sheet's foot is faded out in the file itself,
so the art dissolves into the flat tone with no seam however tall
the section grows. On a phone the section runs several times taller
than the sheet, so most of it is the flat tone.

That makes the first section warmer than the rest of the page. It
is deliberate — it reads as a map page — but it is the one thing to
look at first.

A LESSON ON KEYING THESE PRINTS
Several arrived with the transparency checkerboard flattened in.
Flooding the background from the corners is the usual fix, but on a
photograph with a bright near-neutral sky the flood leaks through
the paper border and punches a hole in the sky. Safer method: label
everything that is NOT background, take the largest blob and fill
its holes — that is the print silhouette, sky included by
definition. A patch enclosed behind a curled corner still cannot be
reached by any flood. Ask for a properly transparent file instead;
it is quicker than fighting it.

SIX SPREADS, ONE BOOK
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Every sheet is decorated down BOTH outer edges at every height. The
usable ground on all six is the middle 20-80% of the width. That is
the hard constraint behind every composition here; a mockup that
puts content at the far left or far right will not survive contact
with the real artwork.

Inside that band each section is placed differently on purpose:

  trail     staggered, left to right, route between the tiers
  falls     a descent — copy left, print upper right, print lower left

  dock      horizontal noticeboard, three blocks across
  sled      one block right of centre in open snow, route sweeping under
  ice       pinwheel across the lake, NO route

Routes exist only where they mean something: trail, falls, dock,
sled. Wildlife and ice have none.

Photo widths vary by section on purpose — 180px for the pelican up
to 310px for the winter lodge. Do not normalise them.

Blur the blocks and the six silhouettes should still be tellable

THE SHEETS MEET AS CUT-OUTS, NOT FADES
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The drawn edge IS the boundary. Above it a section keeps the colour
of the section above; below the line where the artwork becomes
opaque right across its width, it switches to its own colour. The
switch is a two-stop gradient with both stops at the same position
— a hard edge, hidden behind the art, so nothing blends and no band
of a third colour appears.

Cut line per sheet, measured as the first row opaque edge to edge:

    map 4.7   forest 14.8   glade 17.9
    basin 0.9  ridge 4.5    snow 9.9    (vw below the section top)

An earlier version faded between the colours over that depth. It
was wrong: it read as haze rather than a cut, and where a fade had
not finished you still saw a band. Do not reintroduce a blend here.

THE FIRST SECTION is transparent above its line rather than a
colour, so the hero photograph shows through to the mountain
outline. Its pull-up must equal its cut line exactly — both are
4.7vw — and --map-overlap is reduced by the same amount (26.8vw) so
the peaks do not move. Change one and the other must follow.

Re-measure all of this if a sheet is replaced: find the first row
where alpha is above 250 across the whole width, convert to vw with
the sheet's height-to-width ratio, subtract the sheet's own margin.

The animals cannot live inside those SVGs: the skylines use
preserveAspectRatio="none" so they stretch to any width, and a
silhouette would stretch with them. Each sits on top instead, as a

valley floor at every viewport width.

of its 1440 viewBox. That is where the bear stands, so it reads
against the far trees instead of disappearing into the near ones.

QUICK FACTS GRID
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The panel no longer uses a fixed column count. It counts its own
items with :has(> div:nth-child(N):last-child) and picks a column
figure that splits them without stranding one on its own: 8 -> 4+4,
7 -> 4+3, 9 -> 5+4 on desktop. Nothing names an accommodation.

Two width bands, deliberately non-overlapping: 900px and up, and
621-899px where cells are narrower so counts of 9 and above drop to
three per row. Below 621px the old two-column mobile panel is
untouched.

Separators are per-cell, not full-height. Each block clears the top
border on its first row, the right border on the last cell of each
row, and the right border on the very last cell — that final rule
has to live inside each block, because the shared last-child rule
is less specific than the border re-assert and would lose to it.

Adding or removing a fact re-balances the panel on its own. If a
count above 14 ever appears, add a line to the map in the generator
(gen_quickfacts.py, kept outside the site folder) and regenerate,
or the panel falls back to auto-fit.

Browsers without :has() get the auto-fit fallback: less tidy, still
correct.


COLLAPSED GALLERY PREVIEW
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The collapsed resort gallery is the real gallery, cropped. The
masonry is untouched — same columns, same widths, same image sizes,
captions intact. A wrapper div.gal-clip around it carries the
.collapsed class, a max-height and overflow:hidden, plus a gradient
::after easing the cut into the page. Expanding lifts the crop.

The height must sit on that wrapper, not on .masonry. A multi-column
box given a fixed height does not crop, it fragments: the overflow
spills into further columns sideways and you get the whole gallery
in thin strips instead of the top of it. This was tried and was
wrong. Constrain a plain block parent instead.

Preview heights: 240-320px mobile, 300-400px from 700px, 340-460px
from 1100px. Tune these to taste; nothing else depends on them.

site.js was not touched. It reads getElementById("resort-gallery"),
which is now the wrapper rather than the masonry, and toggles the
class on it. aria-controls still resolves.

The gradient hard-codes --paper as rgba(243,238,225,...). If the
gallery section ever moves onto a different band colour, that
gradient needs a matching override or the fade will show as a
pale block.

Cost of cropping rather than hiding: all 34 thumbnails now lay out
and load, where the old collapsed state used display:none and
fetched only the visible few. Roughly 2 MB on a page visit. Hiding
the far-below items would fix it but would also reshuffle the
masonry balance and change which photographs appear in the visible
row, so it has not been done.

DEPLOYMENT
------------------------------------------------------------------
Upload the whole folder, not just the .html files. css, js and
images must go up together.

The stylesheet address carries a hash of the css and js content
(?v=ffe03361 at the time of writing), so it changes only when
those files change. _headers tells Cloudflare to revalidate css
and js on every request.

If the site ever looks unstyled after a deploy, that is almost
always an old stylesheet still being served. Open css/site.css on
the live URL and search it for ".accom" — if it is not there, the
stylesheet did not upload.
