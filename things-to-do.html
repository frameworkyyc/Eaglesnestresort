Anahim Lake · West Chilcotin

There’s a lot out here.

Paddle out from the dock. Watch the pelicans come in. Cast a line, find a trail or head farther into the Chilcotin. And when the lake freezes, come back and do it all differently.

No car required

Start at the dock.

Some of the best of it is thirty seconds from your door.

Canoes & pedal boats

Grab a paddle and head out from the dock. Canoes and pedal boats are available for guests to explore Anahim Lake at their own pace.

See what’s biting.

Anahim Lake is right out front, and the fishing starts where the dock ends. Bring your own gear, take a boat out, or just cast from the shore and see what happens.

Anahim Lake fish

  • Rainbow Trout
  • Cuttbow

Licences, limits and seasons change. Check the B.C. freshwater fishing regulations before you go.

Bring the binoculars

Bird watching.

Anahim Lake is a birding lake. Eagles work the shoreline, ospreys fish the shallows, terns turn over the open water, and the whole thing carries on whether or not anyone is watching. Most of it can be seen from the dock, the deck or a canoe.

The regulars

American white pelicans are one of Anahim Lake’s signature summer visitors, feeding on the lake around Eagle’s Nest. Look straight across from the lodge — that is usually where they are.

Who’s around?

Beyond the gate

Go a little farther.

Adventure is all around Anahim Lake. Use the map to explore some of our favourite places to go.

Follow the water

Chase a waterfall.

Hotnarko Falls

South-west of here the Hotnarko River drops off the edge of the Precipice, a canyon walled in basalt. It is the kind of place that makes the drive out worth it on its own.

Beef Creek Falls

Another one worth the trip out. Ask Diane and Murray before you set off — they will tell you what the road is doing.

On foot

Find a trail.

The country around Anahim Lake opens up quickly once you are walking in it.

The Rainbow Range

West along Highway 20, inside Tweedsmuir Park, the Rainbow Range is the big one — old volcanic rock streaked red, orange and yellow, with the alpine arriving sooner than you expect. It is the regional hike people come back talking about.

When the lake freezes…

Winter doesn’t shut the place down.

Snowmobile

The West Chilcotin is sledding country, and there is a great deal of it. Come with your own machines and talk to Diane and Murray about where people have been going.

Ice fish

The same lake, a good deal harder. Drill a hole, set up and wait it out. Check the regulations before you go, and check the ice before you walk on it.

Skate the lake

When conditions permit, there’s nothing quite like skating out on a frozen lake. Conditions decide, not the calendar — ask before you go out.

After dark

Look up.

There is no city out here to wash the sky out. On a clear night you can stand on the dock and see about as much of it as you are ever going to see.

One more thing

Not sure where to start?

Ask Diane and Murray. They live here.
