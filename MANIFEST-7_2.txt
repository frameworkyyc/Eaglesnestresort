WHAT THE STRAIGHT EDGE ACTUALLY WAS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Not a black box sitting over the picture. The copy block is pinned
to the foot of the hero, and the wash it carried was still about a
quarter opaque where that block began — so the block's own top edge
drew a hard dark line across the full width, at 53% of the hero's
height. The rectangle you could see WAS the gradient being cut off
at its container.

The wash now lives on the same block but is shaped to reach full
transparency at both the top and the bottom of it, so there is no
edge left to show. Sideways it is wider than the screen and simply
runs off, which has no edge either.

Measured rather than eyeballed. Across the row where the line sat,
the full-width gradient fell from 21.1 to 10.1 — level with the rows
either side of it. Sampling narrow columns, the step fell from 112
to 14 at the far left and from 99 to 2 left of centre. What is left
at that height is the mountain ridge in the engraving, which reads
identically before and after.


ONE THING I TRIED FIRST AND BACKED OUT
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The obvious move is to paint the wash on the hero itself, which
gives it the whole frame to fade in. It looks right at one window
size and fails at every other: the hero is sized against the window
HEIGHT as well as its width, so the copy sits at 42% of the hero on
a short window and 82% on a tall one, and an ellipse pinned to the
hero drifts off the type entirely. The first attempt left the lede
noticeably harder to read than before. The copy block is the only
frame that tracks the words, so the wash stayed there and was
reshaped instead.
