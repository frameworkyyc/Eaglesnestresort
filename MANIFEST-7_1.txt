THE BOTTOM EDGE — WHAT IT ACTUALLY WAS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Not a colour change. snow-sheet.webp carries its own fade at the
bottom, but only about half that fade has happened by the time the
section ends — the sheet was still roughly 80% opaque where the CTA
section's background paints over it. That cut was the straight line.

The section now lays its own colour over the last stretch of the
sheet, so the illustration has gone before the boundary arrives, and
the CTA opens on the same colour. Measured rather than eyeballed:
sampling a column of pixels across the boundary, the colour step
fell from 107 to under 40, and what remains is the artwork's own
detail rather than a seam. Checked at three positions across the
width.


TWO THINGS I HAD TO WORK AROUND
-------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE ROUTE COULD NOT START WHERE YOUR REFERENCE SHOWS IT. In the
   reference it leaves the print about a third of the way down, but
   the ICE FISH copy sits in the columns the route crosses and wraps
   deeper as the window narrows — its foot runs from 44% of the
   print's height on a wide screen to 76% on a narrow one. Starting
   it high looked right at one width and drew the dotted line
   straight through the paragraph at every other. It now leaves the
   print a little lower, and like the Rainbow route on the trail
   section it hides below 1200px rather than cross the text. Swept
   every 20px from 900 to 1960 — it clears the copy by 25px at
   worst.

2. THE SKATES FILE ALREADY HAD A TRANSPARENT BACKGROUND. It looks
   like white-on-white, but the background is transparent with black
   underneath. My first pass tried to key out the white, produced a
   solid black frame, and reported a background of 0.0%. Reading the
   existing alpha instead, and resizing premultiplied so the black
   could not bleed into the soft edges, gave a clean cut-out.
